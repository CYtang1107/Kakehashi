## Surface differences and historical drift

Conversation turns are custom flat bordered containers, rather than ordinary shadowed `Card` instances: 95% width, 16-unit corners, 20-unit padding and 12-unit internal gaps. These use incumbent colors and geometry; their speaker alignment and tonal differentiation are local conversation patterns. The white turn's lack of ambient card shadow is an observed difference from the ordinary-card recipe, not a replacement elevation token.

The composer overrides the shared multiline field's usual 140-unit minimum with `80 × appScale` minimum and `140 × appScale` maximum. This is a bounded editing area above a persistent Send button, while messages and control labels retain flexible height. It is a feature-specific sizing difference, not a new field token. At enlarged reading sizes, that footer and wrapping navigation necessarily leave less viewport space for conversation. Source provides a scrolling conversation region; this documentation pass did not measure the resulting viewport or inspect native keyboard/IME behavior.

The older-adult rail threshold (`1100 × appScale`), rail width (`260 × appScale`) and navigation wrapping rule remain Stage 2 surface decisions. Their 60-unit minimum targets and default-body-weight `AppText` navigation labels differ from the Stage 1 sidecar's representative family navigation. Stage 3's grouped toolbar and content-height family/student tabs likewise remain the previously recorded drift; Stage 4 does not repair or canonize them.

PRODUCT.md, DESIGN.md and the sidecar still describe their Stage 1 implementation scope. They do not enumerate this companion/profile extension or all subsequent role migrations. The statement that native **reading preferences** are session-only remains accurate; adding native **feature profile** persistence does not establish native reading-preference persistence. Canonical scope language and component previews remain historical evidence, preserved under this handoff's boundary.
